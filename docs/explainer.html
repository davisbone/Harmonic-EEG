01 / THE EXPERIMENT

The middle chord is the event.

Participants heard three-chord sequences. Most middle chords were standards; some changed harmonic function or chord inversion. The target combines those two deviant categories. Electrodes recorded voltage at 2,048 samples per second.

The question: can 21 early EEG voltage features tell standard from deviant middle chords in participants the model was not trained on? A yes would show that early EEG carries usable information about the chord type. It would not show a pure neural “surprise detector,” because the conditions also differ in sound, attention, decisions and button presses.

02 / THE CLEANING

Cleaning removes artifacts and records every loss.

The release (version 1.0.0) contains 38.8 GB. Its audit finds 28 complete · 2 usable partial · 10 severely truncated recordings. Matching checksums only confirm that these are the published files; the truncated recordings were already incomplete when they were uploaded.

  1. Check the source. Confirm the files, units, channels and event timing. Keep only available portions of the two usable partial recordings.
  2. Process within blocks. Reduce the sampling rate to 256 Hz and filter. Filtering never crosses a splice between blocks. Because filters smear the signal near a cut, events within 21.00 seconds of a block boundary (plus the event window itself) are skipped.
  3. Repair persistent bad electrodes. Detect dead channels and recurring noise, interpolate from neighboring electrodes, then average-reference.
  4. Reduce artifacts with ICA. Separate patterns on a dedicated fitting copy, classify them, and subtract high-confidence artifact components. The automatic labels are imperfect, so some artifacts can remain and some brain signal can be removed.
  5. Check all channels. Make one intermittent-electrode repair pass. Reject epochs with extreme amplitude, flat channels or nonfinite samples. Then apply the participant-level rules on bad-channel count and retention.
What do the thresholds mean?

Epoch amplitude must stay between 1 and 150 µV peak-to-peak on every channel. At most six channels may be interpolated, and at least 60% of source- and edge-eligible events must pass signal QC. These are practical cutoffs, and other reasonable values exist. Passing the 150 µV ceiling does not guarantee an epoch is artifact-free: slower drift or lateral noise below 150 µV can still produce extreme window means. The thresholds were not tuned to maximize cohort size or classification performance.

Why does cleaning need tools from outside the course?

EEG is a mixture of scalp signals and artifacts. The cleaning step needs signal-processing tools before familiar tabular methods become appropriate. ICA uses a 1–100 Hz copy, common-average reference, the extended Picard algorithm with a fixed random seed, and up to 30 components. Artifact labels above 0.8 confidence trigger removal. The analysis uses a separate 0.1–40 Hz band. Removing an artifact component can also remove some neural signal; the remaining data is not guaranteed artifact-free.

Why run the bad-channel tests separately within blocks?

The bad-channel tests filter the data too, so they also have to stay within blocks. PyPREP tests each eligible block independently; a channel is considered persistently bad when flagged across at least half the tested duration. The low-amplitude dead-channel check is calculated across the available recording. Per-block flags and their weights are saved in the participant audit.

03 / THE AVERAGES

One waveform becomes three numbers.

For each channel, subtract its mean from −100 to 0 ms. This makes later amplitudes relative to the immediately preceding activity. Then average across the electrodes in a region and across the samples in each time window.

The same calculation gives 21 predictors per event. Regional averaging makes the features easier to describe and reduces the number of inputs. It does not isolate a single brain area or eliminate noise.

This version leaves out frequency-band power, late time windows, and any “best feature” selection.

Left/right frontal and temporal regions capture lateral coverage; FCmid, Cmid and Pmid cover frontocentral, central and parietal scalp regions. The groups are set in advance and are the same for every participant.

Limits of “early.” The baseline falls during the first chord, the filters use samples from slightly later in time, windows are aligned only to within about 2 ms, and some button presses happen before 400 ms. The features support offline classification of the chord type, but an early difference is not necessarily a pure surprise response.

A future model gets only the 21 EEG feature columns. Trial order repeats across participants, so it can reveal the answer even when the participants themselves are separated. Stimulus names, responses and timing must not become predictors.

05 / THE NEXT QUESTION

Evaluation holds out whole participants.

The next milestone can start with regularized logistic regression and a simple decision tree. Each validation fold must hold out entire participants. Fit scaling, class weights or decision thresholds, tuning and other learned modeling transformations using only the training portion.

Cleaning uses each participant's complete unlabeled recording separately. That suits offline analysis; a real-time system would have to clean each new recording as it arrives. Because this dataset has already been explored, later cross-validation results should be read as exploratory rather than as a final, untouched test.

06 / COMMON QUESTIONS

Short answers about the choices and their limits.

What exactly does one row represent?

One retained middle-chord event from one participant. Its target is the experimentally assigned standard/deviant category; its predictors are 21 regional mean voltages.

Why do fewer than 40 participants remain?

Ten source recordings are severely truncated, and three more participants have too many bad electrodes. The participant audit gives each participant's reason, and the event ledger lists every candidate event.

Why not keep every available feature?

The early mean-voltage features provide a smaller, interpretable starting point. They were chosen before fitting any classifier; there was no search for the highest-scoring features.

What does the preprocessing show?

It shows that every candidate event in the public release ends up either as a table row or with a recorded reason for exclusion, under the stated quality rules. It does not show that artifact removal is perfect, how well a model will perform, what causes the response, or whether results generalize to other music or listeners.

What must happen before we can claim prediction works?

Fit and evaluate models using participant-grouped validation, metrics that account for class imbalance (ROC AUC, balanced accuracy) and training-fold-only transformations. Compare against appropriate baselines and report variability and limitations.
