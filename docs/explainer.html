01 / THE EXPERIMENT

The middle chord is the event.

Participants heard three-chord sequences. Most middle chords were standards; some changed harmonic function or chord inversion. The target combines those two deviant categories. Electrodes recorded voltage at 2,048 samples per second.

The question is whether early EEG helps distinguish these event categories in new participants. It is not whether we have discovered a pure neural “surprise detector.” The conditions also differ acoustically and involve attention, decisions and button responses.

02 / THE CLEANING

Keep the signal. Account for the losses.

The pinned release contains 38.8 GB. Its audit finds 28 complete · 2 usable partial · 10 severely truncated recordings. A published checksum authenticates a file; it does not make a truncated file complete.

  1. Verify and locate. Check source bytes, units, channels and event timing. Keep only available portions of the two usable partial recordings.
  2. Process within blocks. Reduce the sampling rate to 256 Hz and filter. Never filter across a recording splice. The derived edge guard is 21.00 seconds around each block boundary, plus the event window itself.
  3. Repair persistent bad electrodes. Detect dead channels and recurring noise, interpolate from neighboring electrodes, then average-reference.
  4. Reduce artifacts with ICA. Separate patterns on a dedicated fitting copy, classify them, and subtract high-confidence artifact components. The classification is imperfect and needs inspection.
  5. Check all channels. Make one intermittent-electrode repair pass. Reject epochs with extreme amplitude, flat channels or nonfinite samples. Apply the fixed participant-level retention and channel-count rules.
What do the thresholds mean?

Epoch amplitude must stay between 1 and 150 µV peak-to-peak on every channel. At most six channels may be interpolated, and at least 60% of source- and edge-eligible events must pass signal QC. These are transparent operational rules, not universal biological constants. Missing source and filter-edge losses are counted separately from signal retention. Thresholds are not adjusted to maximize cohort size or classification performance.

Why is ICA more complicated than the later classifier?

EEG is a mixture of scalp signals and artifacts. The cleaning step needs signal-processing tools before familiar tabular methods become appropriate. ICA uses a 1–100 Hz copy, common-average reference, seeded extended Picard and up to 30 components. Artifact labels above 0.8 confidence trigger removal. The analysis uses a separate 0.1–40 Hz band. Removing an artifact component can also remove some neural signal; the remaining data is not guaranteed artifact-free.

Why run the bad-channel tests separately within blocks?

The diagnostic filters must not cross discontinuities either. PyPREP tests each eligible block independently; a channel is considered persistently bad when flagged across at least half the tested duration. The low-amplitude dead-channel check is calculated across the available recording. Per-block flags and their weights are saved in the participant audit.

03 / THE AVERAGES

One waveform becomes three numbers.

For each channel, subtract its mean from −100 to 0 ms. This makes later amplitudes relative to the immediately preceding activity. Then average across the electrodes in a region and across the samples in each time window.

Feature = average regional voltage in a window − regional baseline

The same calculation gives 21 predictors per event. Regional averaging makes the features easier to describe and reduces the number of inputs. It does not isolate a single brain area or eliminate noise.

No frequency-band powers, late windows or selected “best” features are included in this version.

Left/right frontal and temporal regions capture lateral coverage; FCmid, Cmid and Pmid cover frontocentral, central and parietal scalp regions. These are fixed electrode groups, not estimated anatomical sources.

“Early” does not mean “only surprise.” The baseline occurs during the first chord. Filtering is noncausal, sample alignment is finite, and some responses occur before 400 ms. The features support offline condition classification, with these limitations.

05 / THE NEXT QUESTION

A new participant is the real test.

The next milestone can start with regularized logistic regression and a simple decision tree. Each validation fold must hold out entire participants. Fit scaling, tuning and other learned modeling transformations using only the training portion.

Cleaning uses each participant's complete unlabeled recording separately. That is an offline preprocessing assumption, not a real-time decoder. Previous exploration also means that a later split cannot retrospectively become a completely untouched test set.

06 / QUESTIONS TO EXPECT

Explain the decision, then its limit.

What exactly does one row represent?

One retained middle-chord event from one participant. Its target is the experimentally assigned standard/deviant category; its predictors are 21 fixed regional mean voltages.

Why do fewer than 40 participants remain?

Some source recordings are severely truncated. Further participants can fail fixed electrode or epoch-retention rules. The participant audit distinguishes those reasons and the event ledger accounts for every candidate.

Why not keep every available feature?

The early mean-voltage features provide a smaller, interpretable starting point. This is a design choice made before fitting this version's classifiers, not a search for the features with the best score.

What does the preprocessing prove?

It shows that verified source samples can be transformed consistently into an auditable table under the stated quality rules. It does not prove perfect artifact removal, model performance, a causal explanation, or generalization to other music and populations.

What must happen before we can claim prediction works?

Fit and evaluate models using participant-grouped validation, sensible imbalance-aware metrics and training-fold-only transformations. Compare against appropriate baselines and report variability and limitations.
